A Declaration by Parents and Educators on the Protection of Toddlers in Early Childhood Settings

Re: Updating the Installation of Cameras for the Protection of Toddlers in Daycare Centers Law, 5779-2018

To
The Minister of Education · The Knesset Education, Culture and Sports Committee · Members of the Knesset
Cc
The Privacy Protection Authority · The officials responsible for early childhood at the Ministry of Education · Daycare and kindergarten organizations · The National Parents' Leadership · The National Council for the Child · The Federation of Local Authorities
Version
November 9, 2026

We, the undersigned, parents of children in early childhood settings, staff members, directors, educators and academics, appeal to the Minister of Education and to the Members of the Knesset with a request to update the law as set out below.

A.Background
  1. The law requires the installation of cameras in daycare centers for toddlers with 7 or more toddlers, unless 70% of the parents object in writing. The cameras record without audio on a closed circuit, the footage is kept for 30 to 90 days, and as a rule it is not viewed except when there is suspicion, and only by authorized parties.
  2. There is no real monitoring of whether the cameras are working. According to the State Comptroller's report (2026), in about 27% of the daycare centers inspected a malfunction was found that prevented proper recording, and in more than half of the daycare centers inspected a malfunction lasted at least three consecutive months. The monitoring system required by the Ministry of Education was not installed in most private daycare centers, partly because the existing cameras do not support it. Since operators are also prohibited from viewing the footage, a malfunction is usually discovered only when the footage is requested, after there is already suspicion.
  3. The 2024 amendment to the law, which the High Court of Justice upheld in 2025, set a closed list of permitted viewing options for parents, chosen by the operator. All of them are based on human viewing of video. Automated real-time analysis is not included in the list, and therefore using it requires an amendment to the law.
  4. Cases of harm to toddlers continue to be discovered late and by chance: a parent who noticed signs, or a child taken for medical treatment. By that stage, the harm has already occurred, sometimes over a long period.
  5. At the same time, resources for oversight and guidance of the settings have been cut. According to an analysis by the Berl Katznelson Foundation of the proposed budget, the budget for implementing the daycare oversight regulations is expected to fall from about NIS 190 million in 2023 to NIS 46 million in 2026.
  6. The current situation harms children, and also harms public trust in the dedicated staff, who make up the majority of those working in these settings.

Sources: Text of the law (Reshumot, 2018) · Ministry of Education · High Court of Justice on the amendment, 2025 · Privacy Protection Authority · The amendment to the law, 2024 · The Ariel indictment, 2026 · State Comptroller, 2026 · Budget: Berl Katznelson Foundation (ynet), 2026

B.Principles
  • Protection in real time. The protection mechanism should detect situations that increase risk early, and help prevent harm while it is happening, not only document it after the fact.
  • Professional guidance and pedagogical support for staff. The tool should also be used for guidance, feedback and strengthening staff, not only for detecting failures.
  • Privacy. The privacy of children, parents and staff must be protected. Ongoing video viewing, or retention of footage beyond what is necessary, must not be permitted.
  • Transparency and oversight. Any use of the technology must be regulated, logged, and subject to external audit.
  • State responsibility. Parents should not have to take action on their own, for example by using recording devices, to protect their children.

C.The request
  1. To update the law by adding a fourth track: automated, real-time analysis of the footage from the existing cameras, whose outputs are alerts and written insights for the responsible person in the setting, and in a serious incident also for an external and independent body, without ongoing human viewing of video. The track will define a technological principle, not a product, and will be open to any system that meets the regulatory standard.
  2. To establish a strict regulatory standard and binding conditions for the track:
    • Approval and oversight: prior approval by a competent government body, external accuracy testing before deployment and on an ongoing basis, periodic independent audits, and continuous monitoring of camera health with immediate malfunction alerts.
    • Privacy and security: information security and privacy requirements, no retention of video beyond what is necessary, viewing of a specific clip only for a focused, authorized and logged review, logging of all access to information, deletion after a set period, and oversight by the Privacy Protection Authority.
    • Use of alerts: use of the information for protection and professional guidance purposes only, an immediate response when a child is in danger, and a prohibition on the setting deleting, editing or hiding alerts.
    • Protection of staff: a ban on ranking employees, a proper human review before any determination regarding an employee, and full transparency to staff about what is collected.
  3. To allow deployment in settings from the first phase, accompanied by an external and independent evaluation study whose results will be made public.
  4. To allocate a dedicated budget for the track and for professional guidance, in addition to existing oversight resources.

D.Submission and follow-up

The declaration will be submitted to the Minister of Education and to the Chair of the Knesset Education Committee, together with a cover letter and the verified number of signatories, upon reaching 10,000 signatures or ahead of a committee hearing on the subject, whichever comes first. Signing will continue after submission, and the updated number of signatories will be submitted again at every milestone and at every hearing. Submission will be made at a meeting or by official letter, and a copy will be sent to the parties listed at the top of the declaration. Signatories who opted in to receive updates will be informed of the submission and of the response received.

What signing means

By signing, I join this declaration and support the request set out in it. The declaration concerns public policy and does not express support for any particular vendor or product. Signing creates no financial or legal obligation.

The names of signatories and their towns will be submitted to Members of the Knesset and to the relevant government ministries as part of the declaration. Contact details will not be shared.

Initiator of the declaration: Aharon Nais, director of a kindergarten network and founder of Ein Tzofia, a company that develops technology of the kind described in the declaration. The declaration is worded so that it does not depend on any particular vendor, and it demands a track open to any system that meets a strict regulatory standard.

      Questions and concerns

      Can the system make mistakes?

      Yes. Any system can make mistakes, so we distinguish between two things. When an alert points to danger to a child, people respond immediately and protect the child, without waiting for any review. But a finding that someone caused harm, or any step against an employee, happens only after a proper human review. An insight is a signal to check, not a verdict, and the external research the declaration demands will measure and publish the error rate.

      What happens once an alert comes in?

      The alert goes in real time to the person responsible in the setting, who steps in immediately, makes sure the child is safe and handles the situation. A serious alert is also sent, in parallel, to an external and independent body, such as an oversight authority or a dedicated call center, so that protection does not depend only on the people inside the setting. Alerts cannot be deleted or hidden, and the incident and how it was handled are logged. If there is a suspicion that a child was harmed, the duties that already exist in the law apply, including reporting to the competent authorities. Any determination about an employee's responsibility is made only after a proper inquiry, and not on the basis of the alert alone.

      Does it replace oversight and staff?

      No. It is a tool in the hands of staff and supervisors, not a replacement for them. That is why the declaration demands a dedicated budget in addition to existing oversight resources, not instead of them.

      Who receives the information, and who may access it?

      Only those who need to act: the responsible person in the setting receives alerts, and serious alerts also go to an external and independent body; the staff receive feedback on their work and pedagogical support; and oversight bodies receive a daycare-level picture of the situation. Parents do not see information about other children. Every access is logged, the information is deleted after a set period, and the Privacy Protection Authority provides oversight.

      Does the system flag employees as "dangerous"?

      No. It detects situations that increase risk, such as shouting, prolonged crying with no response, a staff member alone with too many children, or overload, and alerts so that the pressure can be eased and help provided. It does not predict who will cause harm, and it does not rank people.

      Will the information be used to rank employees or make decisions against them?

      No. The declaration demands a ban on ranking employees, and a ban on any decision against an employee based on an insight alone, without human review. When there is a suspicion that a child was harmed, the duties that exist in the law continue to apply, as they do today.

      What does the support of the Israel Innovation Authority mean?

      The Israel Innovation Authority supported and funded the development of Ein Tzofia's technology, as it does with Israeli technology companies. Such support is granted after a professional review of the project, so it indicates that the technology is serious and has been vetted. To be precise: the support was given for developing the technology, and it is not the Authority's endorsement of this initiative, the declaration, or the change to the law.

      Does signing require using Ein Tzofia?

      No. Signing is support for a policy change, not for a product. The Fourth Track defines a technological principle, not a product: it is open to any system that meets a strict regulatory standard, including prior approval by a competent government body, external accuracy testing before deployment and on an ongoing basis, information security and privacy requirements, and periodic independent audits. Signing does not commit you or your daycare to anything, and we will not use your details for marketing.
